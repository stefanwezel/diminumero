Learn English Numbers

This page covers numbers in British English, which is what the quiz asks for. The patterns are regular, and the main thing to watch is the small word and: two hundred and thirty-four.

0–12: words to memorise

These follow no pattern, so they have to be learned one by one:

  • 0 = zero
  • 1 = one
  • 2 = two
  • 3 = three
  • 4 = four
  • 5 = five
  • 6 = six
  • 7 = seven
  • 8 = eight
  • 9 = nine
  • 10 = ten
  • 11 = eleven
  • 12 = twelve

13–19: adding -teen

The teens end in -teen. Watch the spelling of 13, 15 and 18:

  • 13 = thirteen (not “threeteen”)
  • 14 = fourteen
  • 15 = fifteen (not “fiveteen”)
  • 16 = sixteen
  • 17 = seventeen
  • 18 = eighteen (only one t)
  • 19 = nineteen

20–99: tens and the hyphen

The tens end in -ty:

  • 20 = twenty
  • 30 = thirty
  • 40 = forty (no u, unlike four)
  • 50 = fifty
  • 60 = sixty
  • 70 = seventy
  • 80 = eighty (only one t)
  • 90 = ninety (keeps the e)

To add a unit, join it to the tens with a hyphen:

  • 21 = twenty-one
  • 47 = forty-seven
  • 99 = ninety-nine

Hundreds: where and goes

Say the digit, then hundred. British English puts and between the hundreds and whatever follows them:

  • 100 = one hundred
  • 101 = one hundred and one
  • 110 = one hundred and ten
  • 234 = two hundred and thirty-four
  • 500 = five hundred
  • 999 = nine hundred and ninety-nine

Thousands & millions

Thousand and million work like hundred: the amount comes first, and it can be any number up to 999. There is no and after them when hundreds follow:

  • 1,000 = one thousand
  • 1,200 = one thousand two hundred
  • 1,234 = one thousand two hundred and thirty-four
  • 45,000 = forty-five thousand
  • 123,456 = one hundred and twenty-three thousand four hundred and fifty-six
  • 1,000,000 = one million
  • 2,500,000 = two million five hundred thousand

When only tens and units are left at the end, with no hundreds before them, and comes in front of them:

  • 1,005 = one thousand and five
  • 1,050 = one thousand and fifty
  • 20,015 = twenty thousand and fifteen
  • 1,000,001 = one million and one

British and American English

American English usually leaves out the and: two hundred thirty-four. That is correct English in the United States, but this quiz asks for the British form, so it is marked wrong here.

You will also meet these outside the quiz, which always uses the forms on this page:

  • a hundred, a thousand, a million are common in speech. The quiz uses one.
  • nought and oh can both mean zero in British English: nought point five, or oh when reading out a phone number. The quiz uses zero.
  • Commas sometimes separate long numbers in writing: one thousand, two hundred. Leave them out in the quiz.

Common mistakes

  1. No plural after a number: two hundred, three thousand, five million, not “two hundreds” or “three thousands”.
  2. Too many ands: one thousand two hundred, not “one thousand and two hundred”. It only goes before tens and units.
  3. The missing hyphen: twenty-three, not “twenty three”. The quiz checks for it.
  4. Spelling: forty (not “fourty”), ninety (not “ninty”), twelve (not “twelf”).
  5. -teen or -ty: thirteen is 13, thirty is 30. They sound alike, so listen for the stress: thirteen but thirty.
