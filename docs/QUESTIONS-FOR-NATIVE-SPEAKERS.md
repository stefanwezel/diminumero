# Questions for Welsh speakers

*Follow-up to the r/learnwelsh thread about [diminumero](https://diminumero.com/cy) — a
free, ad-free site for drilling number words.*

Thank you for the review. The verdict was clear: decimal-only isn't enough, because the
traditional system is obligatory for the time, for dates and for age. Both systems are now
on the site, switchable with a `Degol` / `Ugeiniol` toggle.

**We don't guess in public.** Every form we hold is tagged with where it came from —
confirmed by two or more of you, said by one person, or worked out from a grammatical
rule. **The rule-derived ones are not shown to anyone.** The site skips those numbers
entirely rather than teach you something nobody has checked. They exist only so we can put
them in front of you, on this page, and ask.

So this is the ask. Answer any one part; ignore the rest.

---

## 1. The big one: how do you join a unit to a score in 41–99?

We had this filed as fifty-four separate unknowns. It isn't — it's one rule with one
unresolved piece:

    [unit 1–19] + connective + [deugain 40 | trigain 60 | pedwar ugain 80]

**The connective decides all 54 forms at once, and our evidence points both ways:**

- `deg a thrigain` (70) and `deg a phedwar ugain` (90) — corrected and confirmed by more
  than one of you — say **`a`**, with the aspirate mutation (t→th, p→ph; `d` is unaffected,
  hence `a deugain`).
- `pump ar ddeugain` (45) — one person, uncorroborated — says **`ar`**, with the soft
  mutation.

Three questions in one:

1. Which is right for, say, **43** — `tri a deugain` or `tri ar ddeugain`?
2. **Could both be right?** Might the connective vary by unit, so `deg` takes `a` and
   `pump` takes `ar`? That would make our two datapoints agree rather than conflict, and
   we'd rather know than pick a side.
3. Is either regional, or dated?

If a few individual forms are easier to react to than a rule, these are what we'd
currently produce with `a`:

| Number | Our rule gives | Right? |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | `tri a deugain` | |
| 55 | `pymtheg a deugain` | |
| 67 | `saith a thrigain` | |
| 78 | `deunaw a thrigain` | |
| 84 | `pedwar a phedwar ugain` | |

Separately: for **22–39** we'd use `[unit] ar hugain` — `dau ar hugain` (22),
`un ar ddeg ar hugain` (31). Right? And does it hold all the way to 39?

Also, do `pump` and `chwech` keep their full form in these compounds, or shorten to `pum`
and `chwe`? (`pump ar ddeugain` suggests they keep it, but that's one datapoint.)

## 3. `deuddeg` or `deudeg` for 12?

We teach `deuddeg` and carry `deudeg` as a colloquial variant, especially for telling the
time. Is that the right way round? Is `deudeg` regional, or just spoken?

## 4. Money

We had a claim that money is traditional up to about £30 and decimal above. Nobody
supported it and we think it was reverse-engineered from `hanner can punt`, so **we've
deleted it**. What we say now is: money is mostly decimal, but certain amounts have fixed
traditional forms that survive in ordinary speech — `hanner can punt`, `chweugain`.

Is that closer to the truth? Which fixed forms would you add?

## 5. Is 120 `chwe ugain` or `chweugain`?

We have both, unverified, plus the story that `chweugain` meant ten shillings (120 old
pence) and survived decimalisation as slang for 50p. True? Still current, or is that
a memory now?

## 6. Zero

We use `dim` in both systems, and `sero` for temperature and maths. Nobody actually told us
this — is it right? Does the traditional system have a word for zero at all, or is the
question meaningless?

## 7. Feminine forms in a bare-number drill

Our drill shows a digit and asks for the word, with no noun attached — so there's nothing
for `dwy` / `tair` / `pedair` to agree with. We show masculine and say so in a note.

Two questions:

1. Is masculine-by-default the right call, or does it teach a bad habit?
2. We now understand the split doesn't stop at 2/3/4 but runs through every compound built
   on them — `tri`/`tair ar ddeg` (13), `dau`/`dwy ar hugain` (22),
   `pedwar`/`pedair a phedwar ugain` (84) — with only the unit changing and the score
   (`pedwar ugain`) left alone. Is that right?

## 8. Which traditional numbers are actually worth drilling?

One commenter listed the forms they'd reach for in speech — 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20,
21, then 30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 120 — skipping 17 and 19, then edited to say the real
list is broader, especially for dates.

We'd rather drill what people say than drill 1–100 evenly. Roughly:

- Which should a learner know cold?
- Which are worth recognising but not producing?
- Which are effectively museum pieces?

(`ugain namyn un` for 19 was described as older and rural, so we default to
`pedwar ar bymtheg`. Right?)

## 9. Are `Degol` and `Ugeiniol` the right names?

The toggle on the site is labelled with those two words, untranslated, in every interface
language — someone meeting the traditional system needs the Welsh word for it anyway. Are
they what you'd actually put on a button? And how would you explain the difference to a
learner in one line?

## What's on the site today

- Both systems, switchable. Traditional is complete for **1–21** plus the round numbers
  (30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100).
- Everything in between is **skipped, not guessed** — the drill simply doesn't ask those
  numbers until question 1 is settled.
- Traditional practice is **cardinals only**. Dates need ordinals (`y pymthegfed`,
  `yr unfed ar hugain`), which we don't produce, and the Learn page says so rather than
  letting anyone assume otherwise.
